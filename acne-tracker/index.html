What good skin actually is

Dermatology panels describe healthy-looking skin with four qualities: even tone (no blotches or marks), even surface (smooth, small pores, no bumps or scars), firmness, and glow (light reflects evenly, no dullness or flaking). For acne-prone skin that means six things Clearday scores on every scan:

How a spot forms

Every acne spot starts in a hair follicle (pore). Four things drive it:

  • Oil: hormones (androgens) make oil glands produce more sebum.
  • Clogging: dead skin cells stick together and plug the pore. This is a blackhead or whitehead.
  • Bacteria: C. acnes grows in the trapped oil.
  • Inflammation: the immune system reacts, making a red bump, pus spot, or a deep nodule.

The plug forms weeks before you see a spot, which is why treatments take 8–12 weeks to show their full effect, and why a new product rarely explains a spot that appeared the next day.

The types of spots
TypeLooks likeBest options

Blackheads and whiteheads are "comedones" (non-inflamed). Papules, pustules and nodules are inflamed. Dermatologists track the two groups separately because they respond to different treatments.

Marks vs scars
  • Red or purple marks (PIE): flat, from damaged small blood vessels. Press a clear glass on it: if it fades, it's PIE. Fades over months; azelaic acid and SPF help.
  • Brown marks (PIH): flat, extra pigment. More common and longer-lasting on deeper skin tones. Sunscreen every day is the single biggest factor; azelaic acid and retinoids speed fading.
  • Scars: a change in texture. Icepick (narrow, deep), boxcar (wide, sharp edges), rolling (wavy). Creams won't fix these; dermatologists use microneedling, lasers, or subcision.

Picking turns a spot that would have healed cleanly into a mark or scar. That's why "picked skin" is in your check-in.

Triggers differ between people. That's the point of logging: your own data beats general advice.

What to track, and why
  • Spot counts by type. The most reliable measure; it's what clinical trials use.
  • A severity grade. Dermatologists' 0–4 scale (Clear, Almost clear, Mild, Moderate, Severe). Useful context, but less consistent than counts.
  • Marks and scarring separately from active spots, since they improve on a different timeline.
  • Photos in the same light and angle. Without that, changes are mostly lighting.
  • Routine consistency and the start date of every product.
  • Triggers, with a lag. Sleep, stress, diet and picking in the 1–3 days before a scan.
  • Hormonal pattern. Spots on the chin and jaw that flare before a period suggest a hormonal component a doctor can treat.
When to see a dermatologist
  • Painful deep lumps (nodules or cysts)
  • Any scarring, or dark marks that keep appearing
  • No improvement after 12 weeks of consistent over-the-counter treatment
  • Sudden acne in adulthood, or with irregular periods or extra hair growth
  • It's affecting your mood or confidence. Prescription options (topical retinoids, antibiotics, spironolactone, isotretinoin) work very well.
